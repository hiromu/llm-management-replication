# Holderness and Pontiff (2012)

Holderness, C. G., and Pontiff, J. (2012). Hierarchies and the survival of prisoners of war during World War II. *Management Science*, 58(10), 1873–1886.

**Outcome:** Partially reproduced. The focal result is consistent. One lower-tier coefficient and three robustness statements made only in the text were not reproduced. A person confirmed all four; no adversarial review from an independent model family has checked them.

**Common rule (post hoc):** consistent. Rescored with the rule shared by all six studies, written after the results were known; see [reports/common_rule_v1.md](reports/common_rule_v1.md). The outcome above is the protocol outcome under this study's frozen rules.

The findings are in [reports/replication_report.md](reports/replication_report.md) and the details in [reports/technical_supplement.md](reports/technical_supplement.md). The paper was rebuilt from the article text and public original sources only, without an author replication package.

## Data used

All sources are public and unrestricted. They were downloaded on 27 October 2026.

| Source | What was used |
| --- | --- |
| U.S. National Archives (NARA), RG 389, World War II Prisoners of War Data File (NAID 1263907) | `POW.MERGED2.txt`, 143,374 records |
| NARA, RG 64, Electronic Army Serial Number Merged File (NAID 1263923) | `ASNEF.FIN.DAT`, 9,200,232 records |
| NARA Access to Archival Databases | 31 code lists and technical documentation |
| GeoNames gazetteer (CC BY 4.0) | Camp locations for the camp crosswalk |

The camp crosswalk built from these sources is kept: [registry/camp_location_crosswalk.csv](registry/camp_location_crosswalk.csv). URLs and hashes: [registry/acquisition_log.json](registry/acquisition_log.json).

## What this copy leaves out

This is a public copy of the run record. It contains no data and no copy of the article.

- `data/` is not included. In the original run it held the raw downloads and the derived analysis files.
- Copies of those datasets inside `implementation/`, `runs/` and `audits/` are removed, as are saved model objects (`.rds`, `.parquet`), per-run analysis samples and the lists of sample row IDs (`sample_ids/`, `samples/`).
- The article PDF, its page images, its extracted text and the frozen publication packets given to the readers are removed. Get the article from the publisher through the DOI in [../../papers/catalog.json](../../papers/catalog.json).
- Raw command-line transcripts of Reader B (GPT-6 Astra) and of adversarial reviews that echo the article text are removed. Their parsed outputs (manifests, targets, review findings) are kept.
- Installed R package libraries (`Rlib/`), compiled files and Python caches are removed.

Paths in the logs, registries and reports still point to these files. Their SHA-256 hashes are kept, so a rebuilt file can be checked against the original. Synthetic test data generated by the implementers (for example `implementation/synthetic/`) are kept; they contain no real observations.
