# Source dictionary: parental-leave policy documents (CEPR reports and ILO TRAVAIL database pages)

Summary-free descriptions for independent readers. These are documentary sources containing statutory policy parameters (durations, payment rates). No values are reproduced here.

## cepr_parental_leave - Center for Economic and Policy Research reports (2008)

Files (PDF, original CEPR releases retrieved from Internet Archive captures of cepr.net):
- `parental-app_2008_09.pdf`: Rebecca Ray, "A Detailed Look at Parental Leave Policies in 21 OECD Countries", CEPR, September 2008 (42 pages). Country-by-country descriptions of maternity, paternity and parental leave: duration, wage-replacement rules, eligibility, financing, and the computation of full-time-equivalent (FTE) paid leave.
- `parental_2008_09.pdf`: Rebecca Ray, Janet C. Gornick and John Schmitt, "Parental Leave Policies in 21 Countries: Assessing Generosity and Gender Equality", CEPR, September 2008 (23 pages). Summary tables of total leave and FTE paid leave for mothers, fathers and couples; methodology for FTE paid leave (weeks of leave multiplied by the wage replacement rate, for a reference earner).
- `parental-summary-2008-09.pdf`: one-page CEPR summary (May 2009).
- Countries covered (21): Australia, Austria, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, United Kingdom, United States. Not covered: Czech Republic, Iceland, Poland.
- Reference period: policies in force around 2008 (single cross-section; no annual series).
- The journal version (Ray, Gornick and Schmitt, 2010, Journal of European Social Policy 20: 196-216) cited in the article was not acquired; the 2008 CEPR report is its public working-paper version.

## ilo_travail_maternity - ILO Conditions of Work and Employment Programme, TRAVAIL legal database, Maternity protection

Files: `travail_maternity_<edition>_<ISO2>.html`, archived copies of the ILO country report pages (the ILO discontinued the online database; the article's cited URL no longer resolves).
- Content per page: legislation sources and last update; maternity leave (scope, qualifying conditions, duration, extensions, compulsory leave), cash benefits (amount as % of earnings, duration, source of funding: social insurance, employer, public funds), medical benefits, health protection, employment protection and non-discrimination, breastfeeding, and other leave (paternity, parental, adoption) where recorded. Text descriptions; durations in days or weeks and payment as percentages of earnings, as legislated.
- Editions: 2011 (pages captured August 2012, the version online when the article cites "ILO 2012"; Belgium, Ireland and Netherlands from March 2016 print-view captures of the same 2011 edition) and 2009 (captured January 2010; Belgium and Poland pages from later captures).
- Countries with a substantive 2011-edition page: Australia, Austria, Belgium, Czech Republic, Denmark, Finland, Germany, Greece, Iceland, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, United Kingdom, United States. No 2011 page: Canada, France, Poland.
- Countries with a substantive 2009-edition page: Australia, Austria, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Iceland, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, United Kingdom, United States. Czech Republic, Greece and Poland pages are empty templates; Portugal not available.
- Each archived page also contains ILO site navigation and a full country list before the report content.

## Related OECD leave series

See `oecd_sources.md`: Family Database PF2.5 (annual weeks of paid leave available to mothers, 1970 onward, no payment rates) and PF2.1 (current payment rates and full-rate equivalent).

## Not covered by any acquired leave source as a ready-made series

An annual country-by-year series of FTE paid leave weeks for mothers over 2001-2008 does not exist in the acquired files; the article states that the author built such a database from multiple sources, with policy changes lagged by one year.
