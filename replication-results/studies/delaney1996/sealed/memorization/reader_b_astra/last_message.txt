1. Journal, year, volume, issue, pages
Confidence: high.
Academy of Management Journal, 1996, volume 39, issue 4, pages 949–969.

2. Data source and survey year
Confidence: high.
The National Organizations Survey (NOS), conducted in 1991.

3. Number of organizations analysed
Confidence: high for overall sample.
590 organizations, including for-profit and nonprofit organizations.
Subsample sizes: do not recall.

4. Dependent variables and measurement
Confidence: medium.
Two perceptual measures: perceived organizational performance and perceived market performance.

Organizational performance covered quality of products/services/programs; development of new products/services/programs; ability to attract essential employees; ability to retain essential employees; customer/client satisfaction; management–employee relations; and relations among employees.

Market performance covered marketing, sales growth, profitability, and market share.

I recall respondents comparing performance over the preceding three years with that of similar organizations or competitors, using a four-point scale from “much worse” to “much better.” Exact wording, scale construction, and reliability coefficients: do not recall.

5. HRM practice measures
Confidence: medium.
I recall staffing selectivity, training, incentive compensation, grievance procedures, decentralized decision making, internal labor markets, and vertical hierarchy. Precise questionnaire items and coding: do not recall.

6. Control variables
Confidence: low.
Do not recall the control-variable specification reliably enough to list it.

7. Estimation method and inference conventions
Confidence: medium for method.
Ordinary least squares regression, with models examining HRM practices and complementarities among practices.
One- versus two-tailed tests and the reported significance thresholds: do not recall.

8. Numerical results
Confidence: low.
Do not recall any coefficient, standard error, R-squared, F statistic, or correlation precisely enough to report with its table and model.

9. Main conclusions about each hypothesis
Confidence: medium for the broad findings.
The results supported positive associations between some progressive HRM practices and perceived organizational performance. I particularly recall findings involving staffing selectivity, training, and incentive compensation.

The analyses did not provide convincing support for complementarities among HRM practices.

Exact hypothesis numbering, the conclusion for every separately stated hypothesis, and which individual effects held for each dependent variable: do not recall.
