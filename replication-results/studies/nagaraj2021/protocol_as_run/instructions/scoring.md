# Scoring and classification rules

## Freeze before execution
The scorer is code, not a model's self-rating. Before empirical fitting, write a versioned scoring_rules.json covering: field-specific implementation tolerances; rounding and interval arithmetic; sign and inference comparisons; SE-unit magnitude bands by tier and data regime; N tolerance; significance-share and sign-share rules; the benchmark inference policy and few-cluster threshold; tie/grey-zone handling; missingness/failure policy; paper aggregation; compute budget and calibration status. No numeric constants are inherited from an earlier exercise. Select justified provisional constants using methodological sources and synthetic/calibration work, label them provisional and freeze them. Do not claim they are calibrated on real papers without evidence.

## Agreement and comparisons
Compare implementation sample identity, N and origin estimates before looking at published targets. Two agreeing out of three permit scoring on that pair; send the third to code audit. No agreeing pair gives indeterminate/implementer-disagreement. Report dispersion and agreement across the full grid; never average contradictory implementations into agreement.

Compare sign, inference and magnitude separately. Inference bands are p<.01, .01<=p<.05, .05<=p<.10, and p>=.10; preserve exact printed inequalities as separate checks. Apply rounding-interval rules first. For a positive significance claim, stronger supported significance is not a failure; a newly significant reconstruction can contradict reported nonsignificance. Preserve hypothesis direction and tail; do not blindly halve two-sided p values. Adjacent-band differences are never sufficient for discrepant status.

Measure magnitude differences in published-SE units, with relative effects alongside and distinct bands for same-data versus different-vintage reconstruction. Missing published SEs mean this criterion is not audited. A printed-precision or absolute-difference comparison may be separately reported but cannot masquerade as a published-SE-unit check. Printed observations and summary-data matrices are excluded as empirical inputs; comparison targets must remain sealed from implementers.

Benchmark inference is fixed by model class and design: linear models use the manifest's assignment-level clustering; below the frozen cluster threshold, use a fixed wild cluster bootstrap with Rademacher weights and restricted residuals. Nonlinear logit/Poisson/negative-binomial models use assignment-level clustered sandwich inference. IV/GMM/hazard/SEM and unsupported methods retain native inference, applicable diagnostics, and inference-not-audited status. If assignment-level clustering does not apply, do not invent clusters. Any justified alternative benchmark must be declared as an adaptation before execution; otherwise benchmark inference is not audited.

Across the substantive equivalent set report sign agreement, significance in the hypothesized direction at the paper's alpha, median estimate, spread, and the published estimate's position. Report uncertain sets separately. Do not inflate counts with algebraic checks, repeated implementations or identical realized samples. No substantive equivalent alternatives means specification fragility was not assessed, not that universal robustness was established. Influence deletion ranges do not automatically enter the equivalent set.

## Six outcomes

- Consistent: agreeing implementations, origin agreement on the assessable sign/inference/magnitude criteria and agreement across the justified equivalent set. Missing essential criteria or unresolved readings cannot be silently counted as consistent. State the coverage limitations of the deposited data.
- Fragile: the origin matches, but genuinely equivalent specifications fail the frozen stability rule. An origin mismatch alone never establishes fragility.
- Fragile inference: the coefficient reproduces but the inference band weakens under the frozen applicable benchmark. State the actual inference change and any benchmark adaptation.
- Discrepant: agreeing implementations lie outside tolerance at the origin and every equivalent reading on sign, a two-or-more-band inference change, or magnitude; this survives adversarial review. No human confirmation is performed, and the report says so. Missing evidence and unresolved calibration preclude an unqualified final discrepancy claim.
- Indeterminate: an unresolved issue prevents a supported verdict, with specific sub-code such as underdocumentation, source vintage, inconsistent targets, implementation disagreement, unavailable magnitude/inference criterion, or incomplete coverage. Do not use this as a substitute for diagnosing why.
- Not attemptable: a core source is absent for the stated scope or implementations cannot proceed, with a concrete reason.

Freeze precedence for overlapping outcomes and grey zones. Benefit of uncertainty goes to indeterminate, never automatically to consistent or discrepant. Any provisional descriptive comparison is distinct from a formal protocol outcome.

## Paper-level aggregation
Retain every claim outcome. Select the designated focal claim(s), treatment of multiple focal claims and tie/aggregation rule from the article before results. Base the paper outcome on that focal structure and qualify it by disagreeing lower tiers. Indeterminate focal evidence makes the paper indeterminate. Do not equate a fraction of matching table entries with a paper-level success rate or use an unexplained worst-cell rule.

For comparability, report fully reproduced only when the focal result is consistent and no tier is discrepant; partially reproduced for focal fragility or focal consistency with discrepant lower-tier evidence; not reproduced for a focal discrepant result. Keep indeterminate, not attemptable and cases not covered by that mapping separate; do not force fragile-inference into an unspecified category. If the user later requests a separate corpus-analysis task, that task should report sensitivity to pessimistic/optimistic aggregation. This does not authorize multi-paper analysis in an individual replication session.

## Calibration and human checks
Within the selected study, distinguish feasible diagnostic/synthetic checks from corpus-level validation. Cross-paper calibration, a post-cutoff paper sample and a human-reproduced comparison set are outside this session and require a separate user-requested task. Record unperformed arms explicitly; do not start other replications to fill them. Before claiming validated error rates, the required calibration arms are: planted textual/table errors, unreachable targets, null data, synthetic rule tests, title-only memorization probes and, when feasible, a post-training-cutoff stratum. Report uncertainty, denominators and limits. Synthetic checks alone do not measure false-flag rates on real papers. Author code stays sealed under shared_data.md until AI outputs and scores are hashed. Do not tune against held-out nonfocal statistics. A human-reproduced validation set and human double-coding of labels must be recorded separately and never simulated by another AI. Human review of candidate discrepancies was removed at the user's request: record it as not performed and do not describe any AI step as human review.

Every claim record retains tier, criteria, values, outcome/reason codes, native/benchmark inference, multiverse summaries, unavailable items, implementer agreement and trace references. Every paper record retains aggregation, source vintage, forensic findings, documentation/unusualness assessments, memorization evidence, calibration status and version.
