**Recall of Deephouse (1999), SMJ 20(2): 147–166, empirical study**

Everything below is from memory. Items are tagged with confidence (0–100). Guesses are labeled.

**Setting**
- Commercial banks in the Minneapolis–St. Paul (Twin Cities) metropolitan area, Minnesota. Confidence: 85.
- Same setting as his 1996 AMJ paper ("Does isomorphism legitimate?"). Confidence: 85.

**Data source**
- Federal Reserve / FFIEC Reports of Condition and Income (bank "Call Reports"), giving balance-sheet asset allocations and income. Confidence: 80.
- Market definition based on the Twin Cities MSA as the relevant banking market. Confidence: 70.

**Years**
- 1985–1992. Confidence: 65.
- Guess: strategy measured in year t-1 predicting performance in year t, so the effective panel is roughly 1986–1992. Confidence: 45.

**Unit of analysis**
- Bank-year (pooled cross-section time series). Confidence: 80.

**Sample size (N)**
- Do not recall the exact N. Guess: on the order of 100–160 banks and several hundred bank-year observations (perhaps 500–900). Confidence: 30.

**Dependent variable**
- Financial performance measured as return on assets (ROA), net income divided by total assets. Confidence: 80.

**Focal independent variable: strategic similarity (strategic deviation)**
- Constructed from the bank's asset allocation strategy: the proportion of total assets in a set of asset categories (e.g., commercial and industrial loans, real estate loans, consumer loans, agricultural loans, securities, cash/other). Confidence: 75.
- Similarity to the market was computed as the (absolute or Euclidean) distance between the bank's asset-proportion vector and the mean vector of all banks in the market, then sign-reversed (multiplied by −1) so higher values mean more similar. Confidence: 60. Guess: sum of absolute differences across categories rather than Euclidean distance. Confidence: 40.
- Both the linear term and its square entered the model to test the inverted-U "strategic balance" prediction (too different = legitimacy costs; too similar = competition costs). Confidence: 90.
- I believe he also reported a complementary test splitting the sample or evaluating the slope at low vs. high similarity to show the sign reversal. Confidence: 45.

**Control variables**
- Bank size (log of total assets). Confidence: 70.
- Bank age or holding-company (multibank holding company) affiliation. Confidence: 45.
- Year dummies to absorb macroeconomic/industry shocks. Confidence: 55.
- Guess: prior-year performance (lagged ROA) and/or a risk measure (e.g., loan losses or capital ratio). Confidence: 35.
- Do not recall any other controls with confidence.

**Estimator and inference**
- Pooled time-series cross-section regression; I recall a GLS-type or OLS-with-corrections approach to deal with autocorrelation/heteroskedasticity across bank-years, possibly with bank fixed effects. Confidence: 45.
- Hypothesis support assessed by the sign and significance of the linear and squared similarity terms plus an incremental F-test (change in R²) for the model adding the squared term. Confidence: 60.
- One-tailed tests for the directional hypotheses. Confidence: 40.

**Outlier handling**
- I recall some outlier screening (influential observations dropped based on studentized residuals / Cook's D, or extreme ROA years from failing banks removed), with results reported as robust to inclusion. Confidence: 35. Details: do not recall.

**Numerical results (main regression table)**
- Pattern: linear similarity term positive and statistically significant; squared term negative and statistically significant, supporting an inverted U. Confidence: 85.
- The model with the squared term showed a significant increase in R² over the linear-only model. Confidence: 60.
- Specific coefficients, standard errors, t-statistics, R² values, and p-value thresholds for each model: do not recall. Confidence: 0.
- Guess: significance levels reported at p < 0.05 and p < 0.01, with the squared term significant at least at p < 0.05. Confidence: 35.

**Descriptive statistics**
- Do not recall the means, standard deviations, or correlation matrix. Guess: mean ROA around 1% (typical for banks of that era). Confidence: 30.

**Summary of what I am most sure about**
- Twin Cities commercial banks, Call Report data, late 1980s to early 1990s, ROA as performance, asset-allocation-based strategic similarity entered linearly and squared, with an inverted-U result supporting the strategic balance theory. The concrete numbers in the regression table are not something I can reproduce from memory.
